Basic science rotation

Lab Bootcamp

Every lab session was also accompanied by research papers outlining and giving background to the specific laboratory technique that we were going to use and learn.

Nine sessions, all run on the same Rat2 fibroblast cell model. After an introductory session on safety and technique, the work builds through two complete experiments: an RT-qPCR workflow asking whether ascorbate treatment changes Collagen I expression, then a western blot workflow asking whether a single reference protein or total lane protein is the sounder way to normalize a blot.

  1. Session 01

    Lab Bootcamp Introduction

    Aim: work safely in the space and handle the core equipment accurately, before any experiment depended on it.

    Covered laboratory hazards, required PPE and waste disposal procedures, then practised the manual skills the rest of the year would rest on — micropipetting accurate volumes, loading agarose and acrylamide gels, and building a dilution series into a microplate for colorimetric analysis.

  2. Session 02

    Cell Culture & RNA Isolation

    Aim: grow the cells and recover RNA clean enough to measure gene expression from.

    Practised aseptic technique at the benchtop — changing media and subculturing adherent Rat2 fibroblasts — then isolated RNA from ascorbic-acid-treated and control cells using the E.Z.N.A. Total RNA kit, checking concentration and purity by UV spectroscopy.

    Methods learned

    Aseptic cell culture Subculturing Spin-column RNA isolation UV spectrophotometry

    Paired reading Chomczynski & Sacchi, Single-step method of RNA isolation by acid guanidinium thiocyanate–phenol–chloroform extraction

  3. Session 03

    Genomic DNA Removal, Reverse Transcription & RNA Integrity

    Aim: convert the RNA to cDNA and confirm it had not degraded before committing the rest of the experiment to it.

    Removed trace genomic DNA, reverse-transcribed the RNA to cDNA with the iScript gDNA Clear kit using a combination of random and oligo(dT) primers, then judged RNA quality against both spectrophotometer readings and an agarose gel.

    gDNA removal Reverse transcription RNA integrity analysis

  4. Session 04

    Primer Validation I — Gradient PCR

    Aim: find the annealing temperature at which each primer pair produces a single, specific product.

    Ran the first real-time PCR reactions as a gradient across 55–65 °C, reading specificity from the amplification and melt-curve data: a specific primer gives one amplicon and one melt peak, a non-specific one gives several.

    Methods learned

    Gradient PCR Melt curve analysis Primer specificity assessment

    Paired reading Bustin & Huggett, qPCR primer design revisited

  5. Session 05

    Primer Validation II — Specificity & Efficiency

    Aim: confirm specificity on a gel, then measure how efficiently each primer pair amplifies.

    Ran the gradient PCR products on an agarose gel to confirm a single band, then pooled cDNA, built a dilution series and constructed efficiency curves. Primers count as valid only between 90% and 110% efficiency.

    Methods learned

    Agarose gel electrophoresis Master mix preparation Dilution series Efficiency curve construction

    Paired reading Ririe, Rasmussen & Wittwer, Product differentiation by analysis of DNA melting curves

  6. Session 06

    RT-qPCR Experiment

    Aim: answer the question the whole gene-expression workflow was built toward — does ascorbate change Collagen I expression in Rat2 fibroblasts?

    Ran qPCR for Collagen I (Col1a1) against the reference genes β-actin (Actb) and GAPDH (Gapdh), carrying no-enzyme and no-template controls throughout, then calculated relative expression by the ΔΔCt method.

    Methods learned

    Quantitative PCR Experimental controls (NEC / NTC) ΔΔCt relative expression

    Paired reading Higuchi et al., Real-time monitoring of DNA amplification reactions

  7. Session 07

    Protein Isolation & BCA Assay

    Aim: quantify the protein in each lysate, and set up the question the blot was designed to answer.

    Isolated total protein from Rat2 fibroblast lysates and measured concentration by BCA assay against a BSA standard curve. The experiment was framed around a known weakness of western blots: a single reference protein may shift between groups, or be so abundant its signal saturates — so total protein was tested as the alternative.

    Methods learned

    Protein isolation BCA protein assay Standard curve calculation

    Paired reading Meftahi et al., Applications of western blot technique: from bench to bedside

  8. Session 08

    SDS-PAGE Separation & Membrane Transfer

    Aim: separate the proteins by size and move them onto a surface antibodies can reach.

    Poured a TGX Stain-Free gel, denatured the pooled samples in Laemmli buffer, separated them by SDS-PAGE and transferred them to a PVDF membrane by semi-dry transfer. The stain-free chemistry is what makes total lane protein measurable alongside the reference bands.

    Methods learned

    Casting a stain-free gel Sample preparation in Laemmli buffer SDS-PAGE Semi-dry transfer

    Paired reading Nguyen et al., Human adipose tissue protein analyses using capillary western

  9. Session 09

    Probing for Reference Proteins

    Aim: detect the reference proteins and decide which normalization strategy actually holds up.

    Rehydrated and blocked the membrane, probed with primary antibodies and a fluorophore-conjugated secondary, washed, and imaged on a ChemiDoc. Band intensities were then compared by densitometry to judge single reference proteins against total lane protein as loading controls.

    Methods learned

    Membrane blocking Antibody probing Fluorescent detection & imaging Densitometric analysis

    Paired reading Protein kinase Cδ expression in breast cancer as measured by real-time PCR, western blotting and ELISA
