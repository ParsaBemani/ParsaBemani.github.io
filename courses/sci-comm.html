Coursework

Scientific Communication

Taught by Dr. Campbell & Dr. Owen.

About the course

Communicating Science in the 21st Century focused on the aspects of professional communication, writing and publishing scientific articles as well as an in-depth analysis into the structure of academic journals and the process of becoming a published author.

Through our assignments, presentations, proposals and open debates, this course allowed me to strengthen my communication skills in both written and the spoken argument.

Selected work

  1. 01

    Pitch Presentation

    How Credit Cards Work

    For this presentation I developed a 5-minute elevator-style pitch on how credit cards work and how credit scores can go up or down. I focused on making my pitch for a general audience with no background in the banking system, so it would be understandable and engaging for all audiences.

    The purpose of this assignment was to be able to present a topic well enough in a short span of time in a way that would engage the audience and make them interested in learning more about the topic. This assignment was challenging because it required a fast-paced form of communicating which had minimal room for error and required fluency and a proper structure without following a script.

    From this presentation I learned how to voice my ideas in an enthusiastic manner to promote engagement as well as interest in my audience. I also improved on my face-to-face communication skills, including tone, pace and body language, which are key skills in effective communication.

What I took away

Throughout this course we learned about the details and structure of publishing scientific literature and providing peer reviews. I learned many mistakes that writers make while creating their paper, such as being too imprecise and not following guidelines closely. From this I learned that the most important part of writing a piece of scientific literature is to be concise and clear throughout, and that I should aim to have my writing be understandable on the first read.

Where my thinking changed

Before this course, my instinct with any short presentation was to simplify as far as I could. Every workshop I had been through rewarded making things easy to follow, so I treated simplicity as the goal in itself rather than a means to one.

This course challenged that mindset by teaching me that it is very important to tailor your presentation to your audience based on their interests and background knowledge. Moreover, making a topic overly simple is not the best approach — sometimes too simple is just as poorly communicated as a complexly framed presentation.
